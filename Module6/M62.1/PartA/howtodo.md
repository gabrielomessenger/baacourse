## Step-by-step workflow

### Step 0 — Create a submission folder and work from copies (do this first)

1. Create (or use) the folder `sumit/` in this directory.
2. Copy these templates into `sumit/` and do **all editing** on the copies:
   - `Subcontractor labour requirements chart.docx`
   - `Subcontractor management plan.docx`
   - `M6_Cover_Assessment 2.1 Part A_V1.1.docx`
3. When finished, `sumit/` should contain the 3 completed files you will upload.

### Step 1 — Read the brief and confirm deliverables

1. Open `Module 6 - Project Task 2.1 - Managing Subcontractor Requirements (Part A).pdf`.
2. Confirm you must complete:
   - Subcontractor labour requirements chart
   - Subcontractor management plan
   - Cover sheet/answer sheet
3. Note the management plan must cover:
   - Work sequence + priorities + volume of subcontractor requirements
   - Competencies required for each subcontractor
   - Type/number of subcontractors + formal structure (org chart)
   - Operational profile for each subcontractor (expertise, performance, strengths, weaknesses)

### Step 2 — Watch the instructional video (recommended)

1. Watch `Module 6 - Project Task 2.1 - Managing Subcontractor Requirements (Part A)_SD_DOWNLOAD.mp4`.
2. If you need to find a section fast, open the `.srt` transcript and search for keywords (e.g., “scenario”, “subcontractor”, “chart”).

### Step 3 — Review the Well Street plans/specs and list the trade packages

1. Open `Well street Drawings and specifications.pdf`.
2. Identify the scope at a “trade package” level (what needs to be built/installed).
3. Create a draft trade list (adjust to match what you see on the drawings/specs). Typical trades for this kind of work include:
   - Site establishment / temporary fencing / amenities
   - Demolition & strip-out (if required)
   - Earthworks/excavation
   - Concrete/footings/slab
   - Carpentry/framing
   - Structural steel (if specified)
   - Roofing/cladding (if specified)
   - Waterproofing
   - Plumbing (in-ground + rough-in + fit-off)
   - Electrical (rough-in + fit-off + testing)
   - Linings (plasterboard / cement sheet)
   - Tiling
   - Doors/hardware (and glazing if applicable)
   - Painting
   - Final clean, defects, commissioning/hand-over

Tip: keep the trade list consistent across **both** templates (chart + management plan).

### Step 4 — Build a simple work sequence (your “program”)

1. Based on the drawings/specs, write a short work sequence (10–15 steps) from start to handover.
2. Decide how long the project runs (e.g., 3–6 months) and which months you will use on the labour chart.
3. Map each trade to the months they are required (rough-in before linings; waterproofing before tiling; fit-off after finishes, etc.).

You’ll use this sequence to justify the labour chart and to explain “prioritisation” in the management plan.

Example work sequence you can adapt (only if it matches your plans/specs):

1. Site establishment, signage, temporary fencing, services checks
2. Set-out / survey (if required)
3. Demolition / strip-out (if required)
4. Earthworks / excavation
5. Footings / slab / concrete works
6. Framing / structural works (timber/steel as specified)
7. Roofing / external envelope (as specified)
8. In-wall/in-slab rough-ins (plumbing + electrical)
9. Linings (plasterboard/cement sheet)
10. Waterproofing (wet areas)
11. Tiling / finishes
12. Fit-off (fixtures, tapware, accessories, electrical fit-off)
13. Testing/commissioning (as applicable)
14. Final clean, defects, practical completion / handover

### Step 5 — Complete `Subcontractor labour requirements chart.docx`

1. Open `Subcontractor labour requirements chart.docx`.
2. In the first column, enter each subcontractor type/trade you identified (one per row).
3. For each month, enter how many you need (choose a consistent meaning and stick to it):
   - **Option A:** number of subcontract companies/crews required that month, or
   - **Option B:** number of workers required that month (if you use this option, state it in your management plan).
4. In the `Total` row, total the numbers for each month.
5. Quick check:
   - Early months show enabling + structure trades (setup, demo, excavation, concrete, frame).
   - Middle months show rough-ins + linings.
   - Later months show waterproofing/finishes + fit-off + defects/clean.

### Step 6 — Complete `Subcontractor management plan.docx`

Open `Subcontractor management plan.docx` and complete each section:

1. **Purpose**
   - Explain why the plan exists (control safety/quality/program/cost; set expectations; manage interfaces).
   - Summarise your work sequence and how subcontractors are prioritised (who must come first and why).
2. **Contractor organisational chart**
   - Insert an org chart showing:
     - Principal contractor/site supervisor/contract administrator (or similar roles), and
     - Each subcontractor/trade reporting line.
   - Use Word SmartArt or a simple box-and-line diagram.
3. **Roles, rights, responsibilities, and competencies (table)**
   - Fill each row with a subcontractor/trade (keep the same list as your labour chart).
   - Include:
     - **Roles/responsibilities** (scope boundaries, deliverables, inspections, SWMS, reporting, QA)
     - **Rights** (access to site information, inductions, clarified scope, agreed variations process, timely progress claims in line with contract/subcontract)
     - **Competencies** (licences/tickets, insurances, experience on similar work, ability to meet program, supervisor capability)
4. **Operational profile (required by the brief)**
   - The template doesn’t provide a separate box for this, so add it by either:
     - inserting extra lines in the table cells, or
     - adding a short section after the table (recommended).
   - For each subcontractor, include: expertise, previous performance (assumed/typical), strengths, weaknesses, and how you will manage the risks.
5. **Procedure for managing subcontractors**
   - Write step-by-step procedures covering (at minimum):
     - Communication method + frequency (site meetings, email, RFI process)
     - Progress reporting (daily diaries, weekly look-ahead)
     - Review practices (hold points/ITPs, inspections, sign-offs)
     - Contract issue meetings (variations, delays, non-conformance)
     - Milestone reviews (rough-in complete, waterproofing inspection, practical completion, defects close-out)

### Step 7 — Complete `M6_Cover_Assessment 2.1 Part A_V1.1.docx`

1. Fill in your student details, qualification/module details, and signature/date.
2. Answer the Part C questions in the final table:
   - Use `Minor Works Contract.docx` to support your answers.
   - Helpful sections to locate in the contract:
     - **Clause 9** (Subcontractors and dealings with Contract): subcontracting requires prior written approval.
     - **Clause 10** (Occupational health and safety): responsibilities for managing OH&S and ensuring subcontractors can work safely.
     - Definitions and obligations relating to **Goods/materials** (look for “Goods” definition and the contractor obligations about supplying/installing).
   - Where the contract doesn’t explicitly mention a detail, base your answer on WHS/OHS legal duties and standard site practice, and keep it consistent with the contract’s intent (contractor controls safety and must ensure subcontractors comply).

### Step 8 — Final consistency check (before submission)

1. Trade list matches in:
   - labour requirements chart, and
   - management plan table + org chart + operational profiles.
2. Your monthly labour chart matches your work sequence (no trade appears before prerequisites).
3. Management plan clearly covers the 4 assessment requirements (sequence/volume, competencies, structure, operational profiles).
4. Cover sheet is complete and Part C answers reference the contract (by clause where possible).

### Step 9 — Prepare files for upload

1. Save your completed documents (preferably as `.docx` unless your LMS requires PDF).
2. Upload these three completed files to the LMS:
   - `Subcontractor labour requirements chart.docx`
   - `Subcontractor management plan.docx`
   - `M6_Cover_Assessment 2.1 Part A_V1.1.docx`
